## About oso.social

### What is oso.social?

oso.social is a social media platform. Here, you can post, follow, share, and stay up to date on the latest news and events, just like on other social media platforms. But here, you’re in control. You decide which content you see or don’t see on your feed. Your data is securely stored on a server in Europe. Your account, posts, followers, and interactions are stored here, but they remain yours. You can always migrate to another server without losing your digital identity or data.

Our promise:
- No misleading algorithms
- Interaction with real people
- Your data belongs to you and is never sold by us
- Managed in Europe, in accordance with European law
- You’re never locked in; you choose where and by whom your data is managed
- Not-for-profit

oso.social is an initiative of [Waag Futurelab](#who-is-behind).

### How is this different from X or Instagram for instance?

Those platforms haven’t been “social” for a long time. The main goal of the big tech companies behind these platforms is to make a profit. To maximise profits, they control our connections, our content, and our newsfeed. They decide what you see and who gets to see your content. oso.social is a non profit platform. With oso, we’re building a truly social media platform that connects people and gives them back control over their socials.

### Who is behind oso.social? {#who-is-behind}

oso.social is an initiative of Waag Futurelab, a non profit that has been committed to fair and open technology for a better society for over 30 years. We research existing and new technologies, ask critical questions, and explore ways to improve them. Together with users, artists, developers, policymakers, and other social organisations, we design technology that puts people and planet first.

Read more about [Waag Futurelab](https://waag.org/en/about-waag/).

### Why is Waag Futurelab doing this?

Waag is committed to open, fair, and inclusive technology. Technology must be designed and deployed in a way that improves our lives and our society. Big tech’s social media platforms don’t operate with our interests at heart. It is time for a large-scale transition to [open socials](#open-socials): social media platforms that are safe and inclusive, and where we as a society can set the rules ourselves.

### Which other organisations are involved?

You don’t build a new platform on your own. Waag Futurelab is collaborating with [Eurosky](https://eurosky.tech/about/) (part of the Modal Foundation) to design the platform. Together with [Blacksky](https://blacksky.community/), we are exploring the possibilities for content moderation. [Thonik](https://thonik.nl/) is involved in developing the brand and visual identity.

### This is the test version of oso.social. What does that mean?

We are currently publicly testing the very first version of oso.social. We expect to launch the beta version of the platform in January 2027. With an oso account, you’ll be among the first pioneers to test the platform, contributing to its development.

Because it’s early stages, some features may not work perfectly yet. If that’s the case, we’d love to hear from you! Share your experiences via [User Input](https://userinput.app/s/did:plc:crzflbsprcjobktyhtjdated/3myfscso2qe2w?lang=en). You must be logged in to oso to post here. When we release the beta version (and later the final version), your account will remain as such.

## Account

### Who is this platform for?

oso.social is for anyone who wants truly social social media, whether as an individual or as an organisation. Anyone aged 18 or over can create an account.

### How do I create an oso account?

Welcome! Go to [oso.social](https://oso.social) and follow the steps. Having trouble? Get in touch via [support@oso.social](mailto:support@oso.social).

If you create an account now, you’ll be among the pioneers, testing the very first public version of the platform. We expect to launch the beta version of the app in January 2027.

### Do I need to verify my identity to create an oso account?

This isn’t necessary at this early stage. However, at a later date, we will ask for verification to check that you’re actually a human. oso.social should be a platform by and for people, not for bots. If you wish, you can use oso.social anonymously.

### Are there any costs associated with using oso.social?

Creating and using an account is free. Of course, it does cost money to build and maintain the platform. That’s why a contribution, however small, is always welcome! Donate to the initiator, Waag Futurelab, via [doneer.waag.org/oso](https://doneer.waag.org/oso).

### Why do I also need to grant Bluesky access to my data when I create an oso account?

During this testing phase, oso.social still makes partial use of Bluesky’s services, for example for content moderation. Ultimately, oso will operate entirely independently of Bluesky.

### I already have a Bluesky/Eurosky account. Can I migrate my account to oso?

Yes, you can! You can always choose to move your account, along with all your data, to another host. This applies to all platforms that use the same [open network protocol (ATproto)](#protocol). Go to [move.eurosky.tech](https://move.eurosky.tech) and follow the steps. In step 3, select oso (oso.social) as your preferred destination.

### Do I have to delete my current social media apps?

You don’t have to do anything at all. Just create an oso account and have a look around!

### Who manages my data?

The oso server is managed by Waag Futurelab, in collaboration with Eurosky (part of the Modal Foundation). The server is located in Europe and is therefore subject to European legislation.

## Using the app

### What sort of content can I expect?

With your oso account, you can view posts and other content from other oso users. Due to the open nature of the platform, you can also follow any user with an account on Bluesky, Eurosky, Blacksky or a related app. All these platforms use the same [open network protocol (ATproto)](#protocol), which allows them to communicate with one another. This means you can already follow 45 million people and organisations via oso. For the time being, oso.social is home only to early adopters, who are collectively testing the first version of the app. So here, you’re one of the pioneers!

In this first version of the platform, you’ll mainly see short posts and images. Eventually, new features will allow you to filter by content type, so you can decide for yourself how you’d prefer to see what content on your feed. Do you only want to see serious journalism? That’s possible. Or would you rather see memes and funny cats? That’s perfectly fine too.

### Can I send private messages?

Yes, you can.

### Can I create private groups?

Not yet, but you will be able to do so in a later version.

### What is and isn’t allowed on the platform?

Everyone should feel welcome on oso.social. That’s why the following rules apply:
- Be gentle and considerate
- Do not spread disinformation or ads
- No activities that are illegal under Dutch law
- Registration with a temporary email address is not allowed

Read the full terms of service here. >> [oso.social/terms](https://oso.social/terms)

### Who moderates the content on the platform?

Through content moderation, we can determine the rules on the platform ourselves and prioritise inclusivity, safety and accessibility on oso.social.

In this first version, oso uses Bluesky’s moderation services by default. Meanwhile, work is already underway together with [Eurosky](https://eurosky.tech/about/) to develop a European moderation infrastructure.

As we develop oso, we are exploring further ways to democratise and localise content moderation. We will be testing these features in the run-up to the platform’s beta version, which we expect to launch in January 2027. For example, users will be able to assign a specific label to content and accounts (such as ‘transphobia’ or ‘racism’) if they perceive them as such. You can choose whether or not you want to see content with such a label in your feed, or whether you only want to see that content accompanied by a warning. You can also adopt lists created by others containing accounts that they have ignored or blocked.

Waag Futurelab is collaborating with [Blacksky](https://blacksky.community/) on the development of moderation tools for oso.social.

In this initial testing phase, all feedback is more than welcome! We’re using your input to work on the beta version of the platform, which we expect to launch in January 2027. ## Technical questions

### oso.social is an open social media platform. What does that mean? {#open-socials}

oso and other open socials are based on [open network protocols](#protocol) (such as ATproto, ActivityPub and Matrix) which ensure that no single party can hold all the power. Anyone can build an app or host a server using the same protocol. And even better: these different apps can also communicate with one another. So you’re never locked into a single app or provider.

### What is a protocol? {#protocol}

A protocol is a set of rules that allows different apps to communicate with one another. Email, for example, also uses an open protocol, which means you can send emails from any email address to any other email address – even if you’re using a different email server. The ATprotocol (on which oso is based) is open source, meaning anyone can build on it independently.

### What is the ATmosphere? {#atmosphere}

This is the collection of all social media platforms based on the ATprotocol that can communicate with one another. As a result, you only need one account for all these apps. With your oso account, for example, you can also follow friends who have a Bluesky account, or read news updates from a newspaper that has a Eurosky account.

### So Bluesky uses the same protocol. Why should I choose oso instead?

Unlike Bluesky, oso.social is a not-for-profit initiative. Bluesky is an American company with venture capital investors: investors who put a lot of money into start-ups, expecting to make a significant profit in the long term. Despite the open protocol, Bluesky is therefore still a business that may prioritise profit maximisation or be acquired by, for example, a big tech company. Our social media platforms are too important to be left in the hands of private companies. That is why, with oso, we are building social media as a public service that cannot be bought out by the highest bidder.

## For organisations

### Why should my organisation join oso.social?

On oso.social, people, organisations and communities can share information, follow one another and connect without relying on big tech. The platform will (eventually) offer newsroom and event features, community and moderation tools, verification, website integrations, ticketing services and more.

With oso.social:
- You have control over your content
- You decide, together with your audience, where the boundaries lie
- Interaction with your audience becomes easier and more meaningful
- Your data and that of your audience is securely managed in Europe, under European legislation.

### Is oso.social a suitable platform for my news organisation?

On oso.social, news is presented as the creator intended, and delivered in the way the reader prefers. As a news organisation, you manage your own newsroom, which you can set up yourself. You do not hand over control of your content to a misleading algorithm.

In this early phase of oso’s development, we are working with news organisations and readers to create oso.news, a specific feature that enables curated newsrooms for both media and readers.

### I organise events. Is oso.social a suitable platform for me?

Absolutely! We’re already working on oso.events, a dedicated feature that brings back online anticipation of planning events. Here, you can list your event online, making it instantly discoverable to anyone with an [ATmosphere](#atmosphere) account. Here, visitors can let their friends know they’re attending, invite others and get tickets. In the run-up to the event, you can keep visitors updated on the line-up, send them a reminder and keep track of how many visitors are expected to turn up.

### I’m looking for an online platform where my community can connect with each other safely. Is oso.social a suitable platform?

On oso, no single party holds all the power. Instead, we decide the rules together. As an organisation, you cannot simply be removed from the platform, as happened to many organisations on Meta/Instagram earlier this year. At the same time, as a user, you have plenty of options for moderating content. For example, you can choose not to see content with a specific label (such as ‘transphobia’ or ‘racism’), or to see it only if it’s accompanied by a warning.

In the initial phase, we are testing different types of content moderation and exchanging knowledge and experiences with [Blacksky](https://blacksky.community/). The guiding principle is always that oso must be safe and inclusive, with moderation for and by your community.

### As a marketer, I want to reach as many people as possible with my content. Why should I use oso.social?

‘Our target audience isn’t there.’ We hear that a lot. There are 45 million users you could reach via oso.social. That’s no small thing, although the largest audience is indeed still on the social media platforms of the big tech companies. But there, you’re at the mercy of Mark and Elon’s algorithms, which determine who does or doesn’t get to see your content. You’re forced to produce clickbait just to gain a bit of quantitative visibility. Your organisation could even easily be censored there. Countless organisations have already gone before you over the past year. So this is not a sustainable situation. More and more users are realising this and looking for alternatives.

You don’t have to close all your current accounts straight away; a transition takes time. Let your followers know that they can also find your content via [open socials](#open-socials). The more news media outlets, music venues, theatres, sports clubs, broadcasters, community centres, influencers and communities can be found on open socials, the more fun it will be. And let’s be honest: you’re better off positioning yourself as a pioneer on open socials than continuing to hand over control to big tech alongside the masses.
